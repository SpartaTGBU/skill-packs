# Voice, sound and money

## Voice
- Write for the ear: one idea per sentence, subject repeated after an aside.
- Lay the voice out alone first, with about 0.3 s between sentences in a list [up],
  then place each picture cut at or up to 0.4 s before its line [up], so the picture leads and the voice names it.
- Never trust a synthetic voice blind: listen to the full read on phone speakers; re-take any line
  with a wrong stress or number [practice].
- Compare candidate voices or songs at the same loudness; the louder one wins for the wrong reason.

## Music and sound
- Instrumental under a spoken film; keep the voice 10 to 17 dB over it while the voice speaks [up] (our floor: never less than 6 dB in the busiest bars [practice]); duck rather than lower the whole track.
- Judge song loudness on the stereo file, not a mono downmix: measure both with ebur128 and compare the integrated figures; a mono downmix made by ffmpeg reads about 3 dB hotter than the two channels [up].
- Sound effects: one riser and one impact per film at most [practice]; each effect must have a
  matching picture event or be removed.

## Money rule
1. Read the live price on the service's own page or button, today. Do not quote from memory.
2. Say what it buys (seconds, clips, retries) and what a retry costs.
3. Wait for a yes on that number. Keep a running total in the project record against any cap.
4. State build effort as time and number of passes, never invented precision [practice].
5. Turn off optional extras that raise the price (clip sound when the film has its own) before reading it.
