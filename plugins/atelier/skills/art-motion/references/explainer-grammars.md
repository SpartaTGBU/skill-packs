# Choosing an explainer grammar

Use at Stage 1 when the clip is an explainer rather than an art-style piece. A grammar fixes how
things appear, how the camera behaves and what the shots look like, so one clip stays consistent.
Tags: [up] = in MIT huashu-art-motion, from reference-film measurement unless noted; [practice] =
our judgment.

## Decide
Ask what the narration must show, then pick the row. Use at most two grammars in a film, sharing
one theme [practice]. If two rows fit, take the one for which you can write a concrete noun for
every shot.

| The narration must show | Grammar | Camera | Pass test |
|---|---|---|---|
| a mechanism or derivation | whiteboard, or one object turning into the next | pan along the board; follow the object | each line appears when it is spoken |
| numbers or a trend | finance chart | hold during growth, one push to the point | plotted values equal the script's |
| an anecdote with a punchline | storytime | quick cuts, reaction close-up at the end | mostly still frames are expected here |
| scale, "what is inside" | flat science | dive between scales | each shot names a concrete object |
| an argument from real archive | investigation collage | pan like a reporter | real material fills the frame |
| a list, stages or an opener | kinetic type | follow the stress | one focus word at a time |
| a product or feature | keynote UI | slow push toward the part being discussed | one idea per screen |

Upstream names eight grammars; whiteboard and object-transform share a row here.

## Timing starting points
- [up, reference films] chart or text shots 2.5-8 s; storytime pose change in about 0.1 s, then hold.
- [up, single demo, unvalidated] whiteboard about 6.6 s per block; storytime average shot about 2.4 s;
  collage fast cards 1.4-1.8 s; morph concept 8-20 s; keynote spring bounce 0-0.15. Retime on your
  narration.
- [practice] If a shot cannot be read at its length, lengthen it before restyling it.

## Rules that apply to every grammar
- Real material first; code annotates and moves the camera.
- Neighbouring chart shots share a ground colour family.
- A parametrised segment above about 8 s is split [up].
- Before render [practice]: sample every 0.25 s for text overflow, overlap and type smaller than
  about 3% of frame height; after changing a grammar, test a portrait variant and a long-text one.
- Vignette, bloom or grain goes on the whole clip, never on a transparent background.
